      The alarm clock

      The launch alarm

      Fully free at launch. In development, aiming for 8 Dec: web/Android and iPhone first. Apple TV, Mac and Watch are coming after launch. These docs also describe later platform work.

      A countdown while the screen brightens, liftoff at your wake time, then the morning briefing. It’s the front door to Flight Day.

      How it runs

      1. Ten minutes before your wake time, the count starts on the screens set to wake you. The screen brightens like a dawn; if you have smart lights, they rise too, always in their warmest colour.
      2. At T-0 the rocket lifts off and the alarm rings.
      3. The morning briefing takes over: the weather, your day and the news.

      The alarm sound plays on a screen: on your chosen alarm screen, starting with a quiet morning sound and a low rumble at liftoff. If a speaker is set up, the alarm raises its volume up to the alarm maximum. Other screens can provide a silent visual dawn.

      The controls

      ControlWhat it does
      Snooze 10 minMoves liftoff to 10 minutes from now, then runs a one-minute count. The screen goes quiet and shows “Snoozed · liftoff” with the new time.
      I’m upEnds the alarm and the night. The briefing stays. A tap on touch screens; on a TV remote, hold OK while it rings.
      ScrubCalls off the morning. Hold it for 1.2 seconds; a short touch does nothing. The screen says “Scrubbed · alarm off today” and goes back to night.
      −1 minDuring the count only: brings liftoff one minute closer, down to the last minute.
      Launch nowStarts a ten-second final count instead of skipping the count.

      On a TV remote

      • The first key only shows the controls. It never cancels the morning by itself.
      • Back and Play/Pause snooze.
      • Hold OK on I’m up while it rings. Right is −1 min during the count.
      • The TV’s Home key never ends the alarm.

      Snooze and “must be up by”

      Each morning has an up-by time: the earliest of the day’s Up by, your first timed calendar event minus a ready lead (45 minutes to start with), and your wake time plus a snooze window (30 minutes to start with). The header shows it: “liftoff 6:50 AM · up by 7:20 AM · 1 snooze”.

      • When the next liftoff would be less than 3 minutes before up-by, Snooze disappears and the screen says “No more snoozes — up by …”.
      • At the deadline the header reads “Final launch”. I’m up then always needs a hold.
      • If nobody answers, the alarm stops after 15 minutes and the header reads “Up … · no answer”.

      Snooze length, the snooze window and the ready lead can be changed in the Apple apps (Night › Crew & snooze defaults). See What’s configurable.

      Back to bed

      After I’m up, until noon, the briefing offers Back to bed. It returns only the bedroom to night, with its sleep sound. It doesn’t set a new alarm or put the rest of the house to sleep.

      On each device

      DeviceDuring the alarm
      Bedroom TVThe count, the sound and the controls. In today’s setup it’s the screen that plays the alarm.
      Wall panelThe count and the controls; the backlight rises. No sound.
      Other TVsStay out of it.
      iPhoneA Live Activity and a notification with Snooze, I’m up and Scrub.
      Apple WatchA complication, with −1 min and Launch now.
      Apple TV and iPadJoin only when set to Wake room · Bedroom screen.

      When only one crew member is launching, it’s a quiet launch on their phone, if it’s set up for it; otherwise the full alarm runs.

      Try it safely. Night › Tonight › Try the alarm runs a 45-second local preview on that screen. A preview doesn’t prove the bedroom speaker or overnight delivery work.
